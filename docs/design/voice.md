# Voice guide

The CV must sound like Natalie. It is a trimmed version of her own writing, not
a copywriter's idea of a senior programme manager.

## Rules

1. **Use her formulations where they exist.** "Developing and delivering
   strategies and projects", "outcome-focused ways of working", "develop and
   coach teams to success", "a keen eye for detail", "taking the lead",
   "mobilisation and management", "support service functions".
2. **Present participle for the current role.** She writes "Leading the
   delivery…", "Providing programme governance…", "Engaging and influencing…".
   Keep that. Past tense only for finished roles.
3. **Plain, specific nouns.** She says "Steering Groups", "responsibility
   matrices", "RAID logs", "Terms of Reference". Do not translate these into
   abstract consultancy language ("governance artefacts", "assurance
   cadence").
4. **No adjective stacking.** Her one flourish is "creative and
   outcome-focused". Keep that, drop "self-motivated and innovative" — the only
   line in the source that reads as a template.
5. **British English.** Programme, organisation, prioritise, centre. En dashes
   for date ranges.
6. **Trim by deletion, not paraphrase.** If a sentence must be shorter, remove
   a clause; do not replace her words with synonyms. Her phrasing is the
   evidence of how she writes, which matters for a role that is largely
   written communication.
7. **Never invent a number, a framework, or a qualification.** The only
   figures allowed are those in her source CV, or a rounded/hedged version of
   them ("circa 900 properties", "£2.5 million").
8. **No first person in the PDF.** Her source uses first person only in the
   voluntary section; the PDF uses the same implied-subject style as the rest
   of her CV. The website may use short first-person prose.

## Her words that do the work

| Her phrase | Why it stays |
|---|---|
| "developing and delivering strategies and projects" | Shows range from strategy to delivery, in her own register |
| "Accustomed to creative and outcome-focused ways of working" | Her values, stated without slogan |
| "a proven ability to develop and coach teams to success" | People leadership, evidenced later by line management |
| "an effective communicator … relationship building skills" | Stakeholder work, stated plainly |
| "adaptable … a keen eye for detail" | The two qualities a programme office actually needs |
| "taking the lead" / "took the lead" | Her verb for ownership; keep it |
| "mobilisation and management of Steering Groups" | APM-literate and unmistakably hers |
| "support service functions" | Her term for back-office; do not change to "enabling functions" |

## Test

Read the profile aloud. If it could belong to any programme manager in any
charity, it has failed. It should sound like the person who wrote the source CV,
with the filler removed.
