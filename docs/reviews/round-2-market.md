## 1. The 30-second shortlist

### What the reader learns in the first 10 seconds

Name, "Programme & Project Manager", a Nottinghamshire postcode, and then:

> "Programme and Project Manager with over fifteen years' experience developing and delivering strategies and projects within the third sector. Accustomed to creative and outcome-focused ways of working… adaptable, with a keen eye for detail."

That is roughly **55 words of nothing** before the first fact. In ten seconds a panel chair learns: charity, fifteen years, generic. The Barnardo's portfolio and the PMQ arrive in the *last* sentence of the profile, i.e. second 12–15, which is where attention is already deciding whether to keep reading.

What they *should* learn in ten seconds is already in the document — it is just filed in the wrong place:

> "Leading the national New Horizons transformation programme across fundraising, retail performance and support functions."
> "Leading a £2.5 million Department for Education-funded Workforce Development Programme…"
> "Introducing a target operating model, new systems and process improvements to a directorate looking after circa 900 properties."

Three named national programmes, one six-figure-plus grant, one 900-property operating-model change. That is a genuinely competitive opening. It is currently the third thing on the page.

### Strongest evidence

1. **"a directorate looking after circa 900 properties"** — the best line in the CV. It is concrete, it is scope, and it implies a scale of change most programme managers never touch. It is doing more work than any other clause.
2. **"£2.5 million Department for Education-funded Workforce Development Programme with the Centre of Expertise on Child Sexual Abuse"** — named funder, named partner, real money. This is the line that gets her into senior conversations.
3. **"Leading Barnardo's benefits management framework"** — organisation-level ownership of a *practice*, not a project. Rare at Programme Manager level and a genuine differentiator; it is currently buried fifth.

### Weakest evidence

1. **"Leading the delivery of complex projects, programmes and organisational change initiatives from scoping through to implementation, ensuring delivery to agreed time, cost and quality standards."** This is a job description, not an achievement. "Complex" is asserted, never evidenced; "agreed time, cost and quality" is what every PM CV says. It is the first bullet under her current employer and it is the weakest sentence on the page.
2. **"Managed many concurrent projects."** — repeated verbatim as the Crisis role summary *and* as its first bullet. "Many" reads as "I did not count". A senior reader treats an uncounted number as a small number.
3. **The profile's closing adjectives** — "adaptable, with a keen eye for detail". "Keen eye for detail" is a junior signal at this level; the panel is hiring for judgement and scope, not tidiness.

### The heading that does the most damage

> **Selected outcomes**
> • Leading the national New Horizons transformation programme…
> • Leading a £2.5 million… Programme…
> • Introducing a target operating model…

A section called *outcomes* that contains three *"Leading…"* statements is the first thing an experienced reader checks and the first thing they mark down. It signals either that no outcomes exist yet, or that the author does not distinguish activity from outcome — a fatal distinction in programme management. Either retitle the section honestly (**"Current portfolio"**) or put a real outcome in it. She has one: *"moving from fragmented local practice to a single operating model"* is a changed state, and it belongs here.

## 2. Seniority calibration

**Current read: a capable Programme Manager in a large national charity — equivalent in delivery terms to a Senior Project Manager in the private sector, one notch below Senior Programme Manager.**

She has been at Programme Manager level for **13 months**. The CV asks the reader to accept a senior title on the strength of one year, while the seven Crisis years are presented as a list of projects. That is the central calibration problem, and it is fixable with framing rather than invention.

### Lines that pull it up

| Line | Why it lifts |
|---|---|
| "circa 900 properties … fragmented local practice to a single operating model" | Real transformation scope, with a before/after |
| "£2.5 million Department for Education-funded" | External funder, named, sizeable |
| "Leading Barnardo's benefits management framework" | Organisation-wide practice ownership |
| "aligning programme outcomes with organisational strategy, future operating models and business architecture" | Strategy literacy — the vocabulary of a senior PM |
| "Working with the Executive Leadership Team" | Executive exposure, explicit |
| "Trustee, Advance NG … sitting on the HR Working Group" | Genuine board-level governance literacy; currently invisible at the bottom of page 2 |

### Lines that pull it down

| Line | Problem |
|---|---|
| Job title: **"Programme & Project Manager"** | The ampersand is a hedge. It tells the reader she is not sure which she is, and it matches the ~£46.6k market title, not the £64k one. |
| "Programme and Project Manager with over fifteen years' experience… **within the third sector**" | Sector-narrows the whole document in its first clause, against the stated brief that this must travel across charity, public and private. |
| "developing and delivering **strategies and projects**" | Undersells: she runs *programmes* and a *portfolio*. |
| "Accustomed to creative and outcome-focused ways of working" | Filler; "creative" reads soft against governance roles. |
| "Managed many concurrent projects" | Unquantified, and duplicated. |
| "**Providing** programme governance" / "**Leading** Barnardo's benefits management framework" / "**Engaging and influencing** stakeholders" | Gerund duty-headings. Three consecutive bullets describe *what she holds*, not *what changed*. |
| No portfolio value, no team size, no direct reports anywhere at Barnardo's | The three questions a Head of Programmes panel asks first are unanswerable from this document. |
| No mention of the word **portfolio** in the experience section | It appears once, in the profile. It is the word that carries her from PM to Head of Programmes. |

**Diagnosis:** the CV reads as *project manager with a programme title* because three programme-level facts are missing — portfolio value, people led, and realised benefits. All three are either known to her or safely derivable; none are on the page.

## 5. Structure

**Order: keep it.** Profile → Selected outcomes → Areas of expertise → Experience → Qualifications → Governance and voluntary roles is the right architecture for a two-page senior CV: it gives the 30-second reader a summary, then proof, then credentials. It is also ATS-friendly.

**Three structural problems, none of them the order:**

1. **Duplication that reads as padding.** The three "Selected outcomes" bullets are near-verbatim repeats of Barnardo's bullets 1, 3 and 2 respectively — same sentences, same verbs, twice within 25 lines. A panel notices this immediately and draws one of two conclusions: the CV is padded, or there is only one employer's worth of material. Fix by *differentiating*, not deleting: keep the top section as **scope and result**, and rewrite the employer bullets to add **mechanism and governance**.

2. **"Selected outcomes" contains no outcomes** (see §1). Retitle to **"Current portfolio"** if she has no realised results to report yet — that is a perfectly senior framing and it is honest. If she keeps the heading, at least one line must be a changed state, e.g. the "fragmented local practice → single operating model" outcome.

3. **The trustee role is buried.** For charity-sector senior and Head of Programmes applications, trusteeship is a differentiator: it evidences board-level literacy, charity governance and the ability to operate above delivery. It currently sits below a 2012 copy-editing course. Move "Trustee, Advance NG" to the top of a tightened **"Board and voluntary"** section, and reference it in one clause of the profile.

**Minor:** "APM Project Management Qualification (PMQ) qualified" is clumsy (qualification… qualified). "SCQF Level 7, 6 credit points" invites a benchmarking conversation and buys nothing on a two-page CV — cut it or move it to the qualifications line only. No LinkedIn URL on a senior CV is a small but real omission.

## 6. What a panel would ask that this CV does not pre-empt

1. **"What is the total value of the portfolio you run?"** — nowhere on the page. Only the £2.5m component is visible.
2. **"How many people do you lead?"** — there is *no line management claim anywhere at Barnardo's*. Seven years at Crisis as Project Manager also shows none. Her only direct report on the whole CV is an Events and Christmas Coordinator in 2016–18. This is the question that most limits a Head of Programmes application, and it needs an honest answer in the CV (matrix/indirect leadership, partner teams, steering group size) rather than silence.
3. **"You've been a Programme Manager for a year. Why are you ready for Senior PM / Head of Programmes?"** — the CV does not answer this. The answer exists (three concurrent national programmes; seven years of concurrent project delivery; organisation-level benefits framework; trustee), but it is scattered rather than argued.
4. **"Tell me about a benefit you actually realised, with a number."** — the CV describes a *framework*, never a realised benefit. Even one hedged example would transform this.
5. **"Tell me about a programme that went amber or red. What did you do?"** — there is no recovery, re-baseline, escalation or turnaround story anywhere. Every bullet is a success. Senior panels specifically probe for this.
6. **"What didn't work, and what did you stop?"** — nothing was ever cancelled, descoped or stopped in this CV.
7. **"How do you assure delivery through partners you don't line-manage?"** — the CoE CSA partnership is the perfect answer, but the CV only says "coordinating partners".
8. **"What is your budget accountability — as opposed to grant funding your programme receives?"** — see the £2.5m trap in §3.
9. **"What do you know about public-sector business cases, gate approvals and spending controls?"** — nothing in the CV. Given the stated ambition, this needs a deliberate answer (courses, reading, a transferable-assurance argument), because silence reads as unawareness.
10. **"Why Barnardo's after seven years at Crisis — and why leave now?"** — no narrative thread connects the moves. One clause in the profile, e.g. "moved into programme leadership to own transformation end-to-end", closes it.
11. **"How did you handle staff consultation and equality impacts across 900 properties?"** — she has EIA and change impact assessment in the CV, which is strong, but they are listed as activities rather than as risk-managed change. Panels in the public and charity sectors care about this a lot.

**2. Rename the job title.** "Programme & Project Manager" → **"Programme Manager — Transformation, Governance & Benefits"**. *Why:* the ampersand hedge matches the £46.6k title; the descriptor matches the £64k conversation, and both halves are true.
