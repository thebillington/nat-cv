# Market research: UK senior programme management, 2026

Research round conducted for this CV. Sources are public web pages, forums and
recruiter guidance; the full annotated set is linked at the end. Findings are
used to decide content, not to overwrite Natalie's voice.

## 1. What the roles are actually called, and what they pay

| Title (UK charity adverts) | Mean advertised salary | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Programme Manager | £46,586 | £30,900 – £80,150 |
| Senior Programme Manager | £64,356 | £45,703 – £100,000 |
| Head of Programmes | £54,219 | £31,500 – £71,041 |
| Director of Programmes | £59,250 | £47,000 – £75,000 |

Source: WorkInCharities advert-derived salary archive.

The lesson: charity titles are smaller than they sound. "Head of Programmes"
often pays less than "Senior Programme Manager" at a large organisation. The
money sits in **transformation, change and PMO** programme roles, not in title
inflation. A move to Grade 6/7 in the Civil Service, an NHS senior programme
role, or a commercial change role is where the next band actually is.

## 2. Public sector vocabulary (use only where truthful)

The Project Delivery Capability Framework (PDCF) fixes titles against grades:
Programme Manager 1/2/3 = SEO/G7/G6; Programme Director = SCS. Senior programme
adverts ask for:

- programme governance, programme board, SRO, assurance, integrated plan
- benefits realisation, benefits profiles, benefits owners
- RAID / RAAIDD, risk appetite, tolerances, escalation
- dependency management, change control, baselines and re-baselining
- business case (Five Case Model: strategic, economic, commercial, financial,
  management), Green Book, SOC/OBC/FBC, gate reviews 0–5
- GMPP, NISTA (formerly IPA — "IPA Gateway" now reads out of date), GovS 002,
  Teal Book, Orange Book
- transition management, business readiness, target operating model
- value for money, RDEL/CDEL, supplier and contract management

Source: PDCF; Green Book 2026; Defra G6 Head of PMO advert; DBT G7 Senior
Programme Manager advert; PeopleCert PRINCE2 rebrand guidance.

## 3. Third-sector vocabulary (the differentiator)

- theory of change, impact measurement, MEL/MEAL, outcomes framework
- safeguarding, equality impact assessment, EDI, lived experience, co-production
- trusts and foundations, restricted/unrestricted income, grant management,
  commissioned income, local authority contracts
- Board of Trustees, sub-committees, CEO/SLT
- systems change, place-based working, partnership working, VCSE

Charity panels often include trustees and non-specialists, so internal project
codewords must be explained. "Programme" in a charity can mean a service, not a
change portfolio: the CV must make the change-management reading explicit.

## 4. What separates a senior CV from a mid-level one

1. **Scope, not adjectives.** Budget accountable for, headcount led (direct and
   indirect), duration, reach, sites/partners. Three numbers per role is the
   practical rule.
2. **Programme language, not project language.** Portfolio governance, strategic
   alignment, benefits delivery, executive sponsor, dependencies between
   workstreams. "Delivered project on time" reads project-level.
3. **The "so what" chain.** Action verb + context + decision + quantified
   outcome + who cared. Name the lever and the mechanism, not just the number.
4. **Board/ELT exposure stated in the organisation's own grammar.** In a charity:
   "reported to the Board of Trustees' Programmes Committee", "secretariat to the
   committee", "presented to the Executive Leadership Team".
5. **Selectivity.** Detail the last 10–12 years; earlier roles compressed.
6. **What senior CVs drop:** duties ("responsible for"), tool lists as a section,
   every qualification ever earned, hobbies, "references available".

## 5. Language to avoid

Consistently mocked by recruiters and practitioners: "passionate about",
"results-driven", "dynamic leader", "self-starter", "team player",
"detail-oriented", "responsible for", "leveraged", "spearheaded", "holistic",
"synergy". Also avoid precision that invites doubt ("approximately 236
personnel"); round to a defensible figure. Avoid claiming a team's delivery as
sole authorship: "led delivery across three teams" is stronger and true.

## 6. Format and ATS

- Two pages A4 for an experienced candidate; three only for executive search.
- No photo, date of birth, nationality, marital status, or full address.
- Single column; no tables, text boxes, icons, skill bars, sidebars. One study
  of 10,000 CVs found 52% parsing failure for column/table PDFs.
- Standard section names. Dates with months ("October 2025 – Present").
- British spelling throughout.
- PDF by default for charity/private; `.docx` for many public-sector portals.
- Civil Service Jobs usually does not accept a formatted upload: the CV is
  pasted into free-text boxes and sifted name-blind. A plain-text master is a
  separate artefact, not a downgrade.

## 7. Salary and market context for this candidate

Barnardo's 2024–25: income £345.6m, 7,404 employees, 669 services, 359,392
people reached. Only about 39 people earn above £70k. Internal promotion to that
band means a Director/Head-of-function seat; externally, Grade 6, NHS England or
commercial change roles reach it sooner. That is context for targeting, not
something the CV should state.
